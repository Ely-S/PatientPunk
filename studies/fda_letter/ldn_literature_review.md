# Low-Dose Naltrexone for Post-Viral and Chronic Complex Conditions: A Literature Review

*Prepared in support of FDA Docket No. FDA-2026-N-4492 (Drug Repurposing for Unmet Medical Needs), "Scenario 2" (promising preliminary clinical evidence). Literature current through the systematic-review search horizon of 1 May 2025, with secondary reports through December 2025. Claims below were adversarially fact-checked (25 verified, 0 refuted).*

---

## Executive summary

The evidence base for low-dose naltrexone (LDN, ~1–4.5 mg/day) in post-viral and chronic complex conditions is **mechanistically rich but clinically preliminary** — squarely a "Scenario 2" profile. As of late 2025, **no completed randomized controlled trial (RCT) exists for LDN in Long COVID or ME/CFS at the doses studied.** Two independent 2025 systematic reviews found only a handful of observational studies (n≈155 pooled for Long COVID), reporting moderate-to-large pre-post improvements but rating the **certainty of evidence as low**. The strongest support is mechanistic: replicated patch-clamp work showing LDN restores impaired TRPM3 ion-channel function in patients' NK cells. A confirmatory, adequately powered, placebo-controlled RCT of **LDN monotherapy** remains necessary — and several are underway.

---

## 1. Clinical efficacy evidence

### 1.1 No completed RCT; the base is observational and low-certainty
Both 2025 systematic reviews reach the same conclusion. The Bond University review (Byambasuren, Atkins, Baptista, Glasziou, Chakraborty; medRxiv 2025.09.09.25335451) screened 226 records and found **zero RCTs**, with only **4 observational pre-post studies (n=155, USA/Ireland, LDN 1–6 mg/d)**; it concludes "certainty of evidence is low" and "well-powered trials are urgently needed." An independent review (Du & Nguyen, *MDPI COVID* 2025;5(12):198; PROSPERO CRD42025630362) concurs: "Preliminary evidence… but more robust studies, such as randomised controlled trials, are urgently needed."
> Sources: [medRxiv 2025.09.09.25335451](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.09.25335451v1.full) · [MDPI COVID 5(12):198](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-8112/5/12/198)

### 1.2 Pooled effect sizes (observational, uncontrolled)
Pooled across the Long COVID pre-post studies (n=155): **fatigue Hedges' g = −0.74** (95% CI −1.11 to −0.37, p<0.001); **pain and daily functioning g = −0.93** (95% CI −1.29 to −0.57, p<0.001); brain fog g = −0.53 (p=0.03); sleep quality g = −0.60 (p=0.0001); heterogeneity I² 0–62%.
**Critical context:** these derive entirely from **uncontrolled pre-post data with no control arm**, so the magnitudes cannot separate true drug effect from placebo, regression to the mean, or natural recovery in relapsing-remitting illness. They are hypothesis-generating, not confirmatory.

### 1.3 Key observational studies
- **Bonilla et al. 2023** — foundational Long COVID signal. Single-center (Stanford) **retrospective cohort, n=59 PASC**, LDN titrated 0.5–6.0 mg/d; associated with fewer symptoms and improvements in **fatigue, post-exertional malaise (PEM), unrefreshing sleep, and functional status**. Uncontrolled; no dose-response; authors call for RCTs. *Int. Immunopharmacol.* 2023, doi:10.1016/j.intimp.2023.110966. [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1567576923012912)
- **Polo et al. 2019** — the largest cohort in the post-viral space and the primary **ME/CFS** datapoint. **Retrospective open-label, n=218**, LDN 3.0–4.5 mg/d (2010–2014); **73.9% reported a positive response**, with gains in alertness (51.4%), physical (23.9%) and cognitive (21.1%) function, and reduced pain. Uncontrolled; placebo not excludable. *Fatigue: Biomed. Health Behav.* 7(4), doi:10.1080/21641846.2019.1692770. [link](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/21641846.2019.1692770)
- **Isman / O'Kelly et al. 2024** — frequently cited as the most "trial-like" study, **but it is confounded.** It is an **open-label, single-arm pilot (n=36 enrolled / 31 efficacy) of LDN 4.5 mg/d *plus NAD+*** (weekly iontophoresis) over 12 weeks — originally designed as an RCT but converted because participants would not risk placebo. Pre-post gains were significant (SF-36 36.5→52.1; Chalder Fatigue 25.9→17.4; both p<0.0001; 52% responders), **but the design cannot isolate LDN from NAD+ and cannot exclude placebo.** *Brain Behav. Immun. Health* 2024, [PMC10862402](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10862402/). **→ Do not cite as LDN-monotherapy evidence.**

## 2. Proposed mechanisms of action

### 2.1 TRPM3 ion-channel restoration in NK cells (strongest, most replicated)
Whole-cell patch-clamp studies (Griffith University/NCNED) show untreated Long COVID and ME/CFS patients have **significantly impaired TRPM3 currents in NK cells** versus healthy controls, and that **LDN restores this function to control-indistinguishable levels**. Replicated across four papers: Cabanas 2019 ([PMC6834647](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6834647/)), Cabanas 2021 ([PMC8313851](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8313851/)), and the 2025 Long COVID extension (Sasso et al., *Front. Mol. Biosci.* doi:10.3389/fmolb.2025.1582967: untreated LC vs HC p<0.0001; LDN-treated LC indistinguishable from HC, p>0.9999). *Caveat:* the Long COVID data are small (N≈9/group) and the LDN-restoration comparison is cross-sectional (separate cohort, not within-patient pre/post).
**Molecular mechanism:** as a µ-opioid-receptor (µOR) antagonist, naltrexone relieves opioid-receptor-mediated (Gβγ-dependent) **inhibition** of TRPM3 — i.e., *disinhibition* — rather than directly activating the channel (Cabanas 2019/2021; upstream cascade established by Dembla 2017, Quallo 2017, Badheka 2017).

### 2.2 TLR4 / microglial modulation (complementary; medium confidence)
In vitro, naltrexone shifts activated BV-2 microglia from a pro-inflammatory (iNOS-high/CD206-low) to an anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype with a glycolysis→OXPHOS switch, attributed to TLR4 antagonism (Toljan et al., *IJMS* 2021;22(16):8429, [PMC8395119](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8395119/)). *Caveats:* effective concentration was 100 µM (supraphysiological vs nanomolar clinical LDN); TLR4 was inferred, not directly assayed/blocked.

### 2.3 Endorphin rebound / transient blockade (weakest)
The classic "transient blockade → endorphin upregulation" theory was **not supported** by the available primary evidence and is the weakest of the three hypotheses.

## 3. Dosing and the dosage-form / repurposing problem

Real-world and study dosing concentrate at **0.5–4.5 mg/day** (the studies span 0.5–6.0 mg; no established optimal dose or duration). The only FDA-approved naltrexone product is **50 mg**; there is **no approved low-dose product**, so LDN must be **compounded**, creating cost, insurance-coverage, and supply-reliability barriers. This is precisely the no-commercial-sponsor, off-label, generic situation the RFI targets, and points to a labeling/repurposing action (e.g., MODERN Act, 505(b)(2), or a low-dose strength) rather than a standard supplement.
> **Sourcing note:** the specific regulatory facts (50 mg-only approval; compounding requirement) are widely known and implied by the universal "off-label" framing across sources, but were **not** among the adversarially-verified claims. Cite the **FDA Orange Book** and compounding literature directly before asserting them in the comment.

## 4. Safety and tolerability

Across the observational studies LDN is consistently described as well-tolerated with a benign side-effect profile (mild, transient effects such as vivid dreams, sleep disturbance, and GI upset); Polo 2019 explicitly notes a "good safety profile." No serious drug-related adverse events are reported in these small cohorts. (Decades of safety data exist for naltrexone at the approved 50 mg dose.)

## 6. Evidence appraisal and what a confirmatory trial would need

**Appraisal:** Low-certainty but internally consistent. A replicated mechanistic rationale (TRPM3 disinhibition) and convergent observational signals (Bonilla, Polo, the pooled meta-analysis) support biological plausibility and real-world benefit, while every clinical datapoint is retrospective or single-arm and susceptible to placebo and natural recovery. This is the textbook **Scenario 2** profile.

**A confirmatory trial should be:** adequately powered, placebo-controlled, double-blind; **LDN monotherapy** (not LDN+NAD+); with a predefined dose and titration schedule and validated **fatigue / PEM / function** endpoints; and ideally incorporating an **objective biomarker** (e.g., baseline/ post-treatment TRPM3 NK-cell function) for responder stratification.

## Limitations of this review

- **Clinical certainty is uniformly low** — no RCT; all clinical data retrospective/single-arm.
- **Confounding:** the most trial-like study (Isman/O'Kelly 2024) is LDN+NAD+ combination therapy.
- **Mechanistic concentration gap:** in vitro work used 100–200 µM naltrexone, far above clinical plasma levels.
- **Source concentration:** the TRPM3/NK-cell thread is overwhelmingly from one group (Griffith/NCNED); the TRPM3 *deficit* has two-lab replication, but the LDN-*restoration* effect does not yet.
- **Retrieval:** several publisher full texts returned HTTP 403; verbatim quotes/statistics were confirmed via concordant search extractions and independent secondary sources, not direct PDF reads.
- **Time-sensitivity:** current to ~Dec 2025; ongoing-trial statuses may have updated.
