PX-01: Dose records are not linked to the outcomes from the same episode
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Severity: CRITICAL
Status: FIXED
Fixed 2026-08-10 — dose/outcome linkage is now claim-level (P.dose_outcome_rows).
  Claims straddling >1 bin and claims without effects are excluded and counted in a
  displayed audit. Ketamine/mg falls below the evidence gate and is now reported as
  INSUFFICIENT DATA; psilocybin/g retains 3 bins, and all four contrasts span zero.
  Tests: tests/test_psychedelics_v2_loader.py.
Primary artifact:
  psychedelics_v2_patient_experience.ipynb, cell e3b5521e
Also affects:
  Cells 16f4d54f and 5680ff74; executed notebook and HTML equivalents.

Problem:
  The notebook first creates one row per patient, drug, dose group, and dose bin.
  It then joins those rows to "helped", "worsened", and "profile" values from
  effect_pair using only patient and drug:

    dose_bin_pairs.merge(
        effect_pair[["patient", "drug", "helped", "worsened", "profile"]],
        on=["patient", "drug"], how="left"
    )

  The outcome is therefore a summary of every extracted effect for that
  patient-drug pair, not an outcome attached to the dose record. If one reporter
  discussed 1 g and 5 g psilocybin in different episodes, a helped report from
  either episode is attached to both bins. The same outcome can consequently be
  counted as evidence for several incompatible dose levels.

Impact:
  The dose-bin rates and contrasts are not dose-specific associations. Clustering
  by reporter does not repair the incorrect linkage because the exposure-outcome
  relationship was lost before resampling. Labels such as "reporting
  associations only" appropriately reject causality but do not solve this
  measurement problem.

Required action:
  Link each dose to effects from the same claim, source passage, and identifiable
  treatment episode. Define deterministic rules for claims containing multiple
  doses or multiple episodes. If that linkage cannot be recovered, remove all
  dose/outcome rates and contrasts and retain dose distributions only.

Acceptance criteria:
  - Every analyzed dose/outcome row has an auditable shared claim or episode key.
  - Ambiguous multi-dose claims are excluded or adjudicated under a documented rule.
  - No patient-drug aggregate outcome is copied onto multiple dose bins.
  - Tests cover a reporter with multiple doses and discordant episode outcomes.
  - The report states the retained and excluded record counts.


PX-02: The energy_pem category does not specifically identify PEM
----------------------------------------------------------------
Severity: HIGH
Status: FIXED
Fixed 2026-08-10 — the composite is retired, not renamed. Four classes: pem_explicit
  (target must state post-exertional worsening), exertion_intolerance, fatigue_general,
  low_energy. Composition table shows records + unique reporters per class: the old composite
  held 244 records, only 41 (19 reporters) explicit PEM. Every pem_explicit cell now falls
  BELOW the evidence gate for every drug, so the notebook reports NO PEM result; the headline
  moved to ketamine/fatigue_general and the abstract, verdict and conclusion say so.
  NOT done: category precision is unestimated (targets are quote-bearing text that may not be
  displayed, and no human adjudication exists) — recorded as a limitation.
Primary artifact:
  psychedelics_v2.py, ENERGY_PEM and classify_symptom(), approximately lines 287-304
Also affects:
  Patient-experience abstract, symptom tables, screening analysis, and conclusion.

Problem:
  ENERGY_PEM matches explicit PEM and exertion language, but also generic terms
  such as "fatigue", "energy", "tired", and "exhaust". Generic fatigue or low
  energy does not establish post-exertional symptom exacerbation. The notebook
  repeatedly calls the resulting class "energy/PEM" and eventually describes an
  "energy/post-exertional-malaise class" or a PEM deficit.

Impact:
  Readers can reasonably understand the headline result as evidence specifically
  about PEM even though the underlying class may mostly contain nonspecific
  fatigue. The composition of the class is not reported, so it is unknown whether
  explicit PEM records drive the result.

Required action:
  Split the category into at least:
  - explicit PEM or post-exertional worsening,
  - exercise intolerance or exertion-related fatigue,
  - general fatigue or exhaustion,
  - low energy.
  Report counts and unique reporters for each category. Manually inspect a
  privacy-safe, stratified sample or adjudication summary to estimate category
  precision.

Acceptance criteria:
  - No result is called PEM unless the target explicitly supports post-exertional worsening.
  - The headline contrast is rerun for explicit PEM and general fatigue separately.
  - Category definitions and precedence rules are documented.
  - The conclusion follows the result from the narrower category rather than the old composite.


PX-03: Symptom classification is single-label and order-dependent
----------------------------------------------------------------
Severity: HIGH
Status: FIXED
Fixed 2026-08-10 — symptom_labels() returns every matching class; effects carry sx_<class>
  indicators and n_symptom_labels. classify_symptom() keeps one display label as the most
  specific match under a precedence documented in the loader and fixed independently of any
  outcome. 75 effect records match >1 class and this is displayed. §3.3 reruns the headline
  contrast under multilabel assignment (-38.1 pp vs -46.4 pp; same sign, same cell).
  Tests cover two- and three-class targets and precedence order.
Primary artifact:
  psychedelics_v2.py, classify_symptom(), approximately lines 294-304

Problem:
  classify_symptom() checks MOOD_COGNITIVE first, ENERGY_PEM second, and PAIN
  third, returning after the first match. A target mentioning both brain fog and
  fatigue is assigned only to mood_cognitive. A target mentioning mood and pain
  similarly loses its pain membership. The ordering, rather than the text alone,
  determines the analytical category.

Impact:
  Multi-symptom long-COVID descriptions are likely common. The current rule can
  systematically move fatigue-containing targets out of energy_pem, alter cell
  denominators, and change the highlighted drug-symptom contrasts.

Required action:
  Use multilabel symptom indicators, or define and justify a mutually exclusive
  adjudication hierarchy before viewing outcomes. If a single category is
  required for display, preserve multilabel indicators for sensitivity analyses.

Acceptance criteria:
  - Unit tests cover targets matching two and three symptom classes.
  - The report quantifies how many records match multiple classes.
  - Headline contrasts are rerun with multilabel and exclusive classifications.
  - Any material change is reflected in the conclusion.


PX-04: The "rest of corpus" comparator does not isolate symptom heterogeneity
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Severity: HIGH
Status: FIXED
Fixed 2026-08-10 — the omnibus "rest of corpus" contrast is gone. Every headline contrast now
  moves one factor: within drug (symptom class vs the same drug's other classes) and within
  symptom (drug vs the other drugs in the class). Both are displayed with reference rows and
  reporters. A reporter-clustered GEE with drug x symptom interaction is reported alongside:
  the general-fatigue main effect is -1.50 log-odds (-2.53 to -0.46) while the
  ketamine-by-fatigue interaction is -1.07 (-2.38 to +0.24), so the conclusion attributes the
  result to the symptom class and calls ketamine the sharpest case, not a separate finding.
Primary artifact:
  psychedelics_v2_patient_experience.ipynb, symptom comparison beginning near
  cell 3d9bbbf7

Problem:
  A focal drug-symptom cell, such as ketamine/energy_pem, is compared with every
  other drug-symptom row. The reference therefore changes drug, symptom,
  indication, route, setting, access pattern, and reporter composition at the
  same time.

Impact:
  The displayed -28.1 percentage-point difference cannot be attributed to the
  energy/PEM symptom class. It may reflect ketamine context, the mix of other
  symptoms, or the way outcomes are reported in supervised treatment.

Required action:
  Replace the omnibus "rest" contrast with interpretable estimands:
  - within-ketamine energy/fatigue versus other symptoms,
  - within-energy/fatigue ketamine versus other drugs,
  - a reporter-clustered model with drug, symptom, and drug-by-symptom interaction.
  Define the primary estimand before rerunning the analysis.

Acceptance criteria:
  - Every headline contrast changes one primary factor at a time or is model-adjusted.
  - Reference-group composition and counts are shown.
  - Drug-by-symptom interaction uncertainty is reported.
  - The conclusion no longer attributes an omnibus difference solely to symptom class.


PX-05: Multi-contrast interval screening is functioning as hypothesis testing
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Severity: HIGH
Status: Open
Note 2026-08-10: still open, and now larger. The one-factor rework (PX-04) replaced 22 gated
  contrasts with 44, and the screen reports "8 of 44 gated contrasts have a difference interval
  clear of zero" with no multiplicity adjustment. The count of attempted contrasts is at least
  printed alongside the selected ones.
Primary artifacts:
  Patient-experience cells 0b9cf0c6, 8aa4635d, and cfe9ea3a

Problem:
  The notebook evaluates 22 gated contrasts, selects those whose 95% intervals
  exclude zero, ranks them, and calls selected patterns "clear" or worth
  investigation. Operationally, this is inferential screening even though the
  text says no hypothesis tests are performed.

Impact:
  With many inspected contrasts, at least one apparently clear interval can
  arise by chance. Selecting the strongest result after examining all cells also
  makes its unadjusted interval too optimistic for the selection process.

Required action:
  Label the screen explicitly as exploratory. Prefer a small prespecified set of
  primary contrasts. For a broad screen, use simultaneous bootstrap intervals,
  family-wise correction, or false-discovery-rate control as appropriate.

Acceptance criteria:
  - The number of attempted contrasts is reported wherever selected results appear.
  - Primary and exploratory contrasts are clearly separated.
  - Multiplicity-adjusted uncertainty is supplied for broad screening.
  - "Clear" is not used solely because an unadjusted interval excludes zero.


PX-06: Evidence tiers ignore uncertainty in the comparator
----------------------------------------------------------
Severity: HIGH
Status: FIXED
Fixed 2026-08-10 — tiers are built from the within-drug contrast's own bootstrap
  distribution, so each resample recomputes focal and reference arms together and an account
  with rows on both sides stays in one cluster. Tier labels renamed to name the reference
  ("Above/Below/Consistent with the drug's other symptom classes") and are defined in §10
  independently of the observed results.
Primary artifact:
  Patient-experience cell cfe9ea3a

Problem:
  The tiering logic compares a focal rate interval with a fixed baseline rate.
  That treats the baseline as known without error and does not account for
  covariance between the focal group and the reference data.

Impact:
  Cells can be assigned stronger evidence tiers than warranted. This is
  especially problematic when a focal cell is part of the baseline or when the
  same reporters contribute to both sides.

Required action:
  Construct tiers from the bootstrap distribution of the actual contrast. Each
  resample must recompute both focal and comparator rates while preserving the
  reporter cluster.

Acceptance criteria:
  - Tier assignment uses a contrast interval, not a marginal interval.
  - Both sides are recomputed inside each clustered resample.
  - Overlapping reporters are kept in the same bootstrap cluster.
  - Tier definitions are documented independently of observed results.

PX-09: "Any helped" is sensitive to the number of extracted opportunities
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Severity: HIGH
Status: Open
Primary artifact:
  psychedelics_v2.py, patient_drug_effects(), approximately lines 397-405

Problem:
  A pair is marked any_helped if at least one of its effect records is helped.
  More records create more opportunities to satisfy that condition. Record counts
  vary substantially across reporters and drugs, with extreme prolific cases.

Impact:
  Higher any-helped rates can partly reflect more text or more extracted targets
  rather than a difference in experience. The same opportunity effect applies to
  any-worsened, though base rates differ.

Required action:
  Report outcome rates stratified by effect-record count. Add a sensitivity
  analysis using one prespecified effect per pair, standardized record counts, or
  a clustered event-level model. Treat prolific outliers explicitly.

Acceptance criteria:
  - Distribution of n_effects per pair is reported by drug.
  - Headline rates are shown for one-record pairs and capped-record scenarios.
  - Results are not interpreted as comparable across drugs without addressing opportunity count.
  - The handling of prolific reporters is prespecified.

  - Any equivalence claim includes a prespecified margin and method.
  - Multiple drug comparisons are handled consistently.

PX-16: Wilson intervals have a narrower interpretation than the prose suggests
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Severity: MEDIUM
Status: Open
Primary artifact:
  psychedelics_v2.py, wilson(), approximately lines 381-386

Problem:
  Wilson intervals quantify conditional binomial variation under assumptions
  that do not cover convenience sampling, missing outcomes, cross-drug reporter
  dependence, extraction variation, or contrast selection.

Impact:
  The narrow intervals can visually imply much greater certainty than the study
  supports. They are not population-representative uncertainty intervals for
  people with long COVID or even all Reddit users.

Required action:
  Label Wilson intervals as conditional descriptive intervals. Use clustered
  bootstrap intervals for reporter-dependent contrasts and accompany them with
  missingness and classification sensitivity analyses.

Acceptance criteria:
  - Every interval type and its uncertainty scope are stated.
  - Population inference is explicitly disclaimed.
  - Cross-drug and subgroup contrasts use reporter-aware uncertainty.
  - Sensitivity uncertainty is not visually conflated with sampling uncertainty.
