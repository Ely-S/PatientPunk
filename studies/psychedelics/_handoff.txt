happened, in two places:

2. NON-NEGOTIABLE RESEARCH AND PRIVACY CONSTRAINTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Read `data/full_corpus_2026-07-31/README.md` before making claims from these data.

  - This is a self-selected Reddit reporting cohort, not a clinical cohort.
  - No efficacy, causal, incidence, or dose-response-over-time claims.
  - Do not infer chronology from timestamps. Duration must be explicitly stated.
  - An adverse-event percentage means "share of extractable reports mentioning
    the event," never incidence. Always state the denominator.
  - Do not turn silence into "no adverse events." The schema distinguishes:
      reported / explicit_none / not_stated.
  - Do not force one dose/effect/duration into one patient-drug summary. A person
    can describe repeated or contradictory exposures. Extract source-level events.
  - Do not assign a collective "stack" outcome to a drug unless the source
    specifically attributes it to that drug.
  - Quotes plus hashed authors plus Reddit source IDs are potentially
    re-identifiable. Quote-bearing artifacts must remain in
    `data/psychedelics_pharmacology/`, which is gitignored through `data/*`.
    Do not commit them and do not produce a public quote dataset.
    NOTE: `pilot_review.csv` now embeds raw source text by design (§5). It is
    private runtime data. Never commit it, never paste it into a report.
  - The extracted full-corpus README says individual LLM records are unstable:
    two identical passes agreed on field set only 36.5% of the time and values
    58.8% of the time. Use this only for cohort aggregates after validation; no
    per-person stories or paired public quotes.

4. THE EXTRACTION CONTRACT (SCHEMA v2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ExtractionEnvelope:
    target_drug
    events[]

  ExposureEvent:
    source_window_id, source_id, source_type
    exposure_quote
    subject                     self | other | unclear          <- MODEL DECIDES
    subject_quote                                               <- required
    exposure_status             actual_use | planned_or_considered
                                | declined_or_never | unclear   <- MODEL DECIDES
    exposure_status_quote                                       <- required
    doses[]
    effects[]
    adverse_event_status        reported | explicit_none | not_stated
    adverse_event_status_quote  (required only for explicit_none)
    adverse_events[]

The model emits an event for EVERY passage discussing the target drug, including
plans, refusals, and other people's use. Those non-included events are kept
deliberately: they are the denominator showing what was considered and set aside.
`ExposureEvent.is_included_exposure` is true only for subject=self AND
exposure_status=actual_use.

`validate_extraction()` now performs MECHANICAL checks only:

  - JSON/schema validity, unknown keys, enum/range errors, duplicate events;
  - target_drug matches the requested drug;
  - source_window_id belongs to the unit, and source type/ID agree with it;
  - EVERY evidence quote is non-empty and tied to its own source window; quotes
    may be normalized or paraphrased, so analyst review checks faithfulness rather
    than exact substring identity;
  - placeholder strings ("not specified", "unknown", ...) outside quotes;
  - structural consistency of the model's own labels: a non-included event may
    not carry doses, effects, or adverse events, and must be not_stated;
  - adverse-event list/status consistency.

It does NOT judge language. If the model says subject=self, that stands, and the
pilot measures whether it was right. Effects still keep `direction` separate from
optional 0-10 `magnitude_0_10`; a severe worsening is not magnitude zero.

Raw text is wrapped as untrusted `<patient_text>` and authored source delimiters
are neutralized. `prompt_sha` covers the system prompt, Pydantic schema,
user-prompt template, source-header template, prompt version, and schema version.
`validator_sha` (new) covers the remaining mechanical guards; it is part of
`run_identity` but deliberately NOT part of `unit_key`, because a unit is the same
work regardless of how its output is judged, and the cache key is prompt-based.

7. THE EXPOSURE-GUARD AUDIT (EVIDENCE FOR §0; DO NOT REPEAT THIS WORK)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Kept so nobody re-litigates the regex approach.

Sentence-level audit over the 2,666 windows of the OLD dry run: of 3,033
first-person sentences naming a target drug, the old guards accepted 823,
rejected 2,117 for having no recognized use verb, and rejected 93 that had a use
verb but tripped the non-exposure pattern. Inspection of that last bucket showed
the guard was wrong in most of them, because bare modals fire anywhere in a
sentence:

  "I have been microdosing psilocybin for years and the mental health benefits
   have been much greater than anything I would ever have imagined possible."
  "Recently started microdosing magic mushrooms and I could feel the cognitive
   function return."
  "Every time my body feels like its going to relapse, I do shrooms."
  "when I used to shroom daily in 2020 at times would take 1g"

Three rounds of narrowing the patterns (restricting modals to adjacency with a
use verb, splitting intent verbs from use verbs, dropping "will" and "can") moved
window-level groundability from 795/2,666 (29.8%) to 974/2,666 (36.5%), but each
round traded one error class for another and never eliminated the irreducible
case -- a single sentence that denies one drug and affirms the target:

  "while I haven't tried ECT, I have tried ketamine infusions combined with TMS
   and had amazing results"

That is a parsing problem, not a pattern problem. It is the model's job. The
whole exercise was discarded; only the lesson is kept.
