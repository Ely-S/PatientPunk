2. THE CENTRAL DESIGN DECISION (do not undo this without understanding it)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Prior work in this repo (../../psilocybin_long_covid_article_outline.md and
../../data/full_corpus_2026-07-31/analysis/psilocybin_analysis.py) reported
"psilocybin 81% helped vs 67% corpus-wide." That comparison is confounded. The
people who write about psychedelics are not the corpus average — they are sicker,
carry far heavier mood/cognitive symptom loads, and name a median of 7 treatments
each. The number measures WHO REPORTS, not what the drug did. The prior work tried
to patch this with a homeopathy placebo floor (n=62), which is too small to carry
the claim.

This study replaces that with a WITHIN-PATIENT design, at the user's direction:
every psychedelic outcome is compared to the SAME PATIENT's outcomes for their own
other treatments, via conditional (fixed-effects) logistic regression.

Why this specifically works on this dataset: the run README documents two biases —
the extractor over-calls positive, and stacked treatments can inherit a collective
outcome. Both apply to BOTH arms inside the same record, so they largely cancel in
a within-patient contrast instead of inflating one arm. Patient optimism, illness
severity, subreddit membership and writing style are absorbed by the fixed effect.

The user explicitly chose this over a placebo-floor comparator. Their words:
"Compare the Drugs to One Another, and to other drugs these same patients have tried."

5. WHAT WAS DONE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Cohort construction
  - Arms assigned by regex over the DRUG SLOT of each `drug: outcome: symptom`
    triple only — never over the whole record. Nothing in this dataset is
    canonicalized (18,101 distinct treatment strings; ldn / low dose naltrexone /
    low-dose naltrexone are three separate strings).
  - Psilocybin patterns copied VERBATIM from the prior run's psilocybin_analysis.py
    so the arm reproduces exactly. Confirmed: mention_cohort() returns 538, the
    prior run's published number.
  - Functional mushrooms (lion's mane, reishi, chaga, cordyceps, turkey tail,
    maitake, shiitake) are excluded before any mushroom test.
  - Ketamine needs BOTH \bketamine\b AND \besketamine\b — the word boundary in the
    first does not match "esketamine".
  - Bare `acid` MUST NEVER match LSD. alpha lipoic acid, ascorbic acid, folic acid,
    amino acids, acid reflux are all common here and would swamp a 92-mention arm.
    There is an assertion fixture for this in Methods §1.2. Do not weaken it.
  - Two ambiguity buckets (bare "mushroom", bare "acid") are counted, reported,
    then excluded from all inferential fits, and folded back in as a sensitivity.

Two denominators exist and must not be conflated:
  - "names the drug anywhere" (treatment_outcome / medications /
    alternative_treatments): psilocybin 538, ketamine 525, LSD 94
  - "has a scored outcome triple" — the analysis cohort: psilocybin 427/717,
    ketamine 422/677, LSD 67/92
  Reconciliation table is Methods §1.3.

Analyses (all in the notebook, all executing clean)
  §2  descriptive rates with Wilson CIs + the community's indication map
  §3  head-to-head between arms (Fisher when cells < 5)
  §4  PRIMARY within-patient conditional logit + unmodelled paired companion
  §4.3 pre-registered symptom-class interaction
  §4.4 preparation/route read off the drug slot (descriptive only)
  §4.5 harm profile — raw counts against stated denominators, never rates
  §5  five sensitivity re-fits
  §6  Holm correction over the 7 pre-registered tests
  §7  conclusion, split into "supported" and "not supported, do not claim"

6. RESULTS (as of the committed execution)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

COHORT
  corpus                      69,161 patients / 204,417 outcome triples parsed
                              (matches the run README exactly; 0 unparseable)
  within-patient analysis set 745 patients / 10,579 rows / 577 informative strata

PRIMARY — within-patient, reference = the same patient's other treatments
  psilocybin   OR 1.84  [1.44, 2.34]   p = 8.9e-07   <- survives Holm
  ketamine     OR 1.22  [0.98, 1.53]   p = 7.9e-02   <- null
  LSD          OR 0.66  [0.37, 1.17]   p = 1.5e-01   <- underpowered, MDE ~19pp

INTERACTION — psychedelic x energy/PEM vs mood/cognitive
  OR 0.22   LR chi2(1) = 22.4   p = 2.2e-06          <- survives Holm

RAW (uncontrolled, for contrast with the above)
  psilocybin 81.0% helped | ketamine 69.4% | LSD 69.6% | corpus-wide 67.1%

THE THREE HEADLINE FINDINGS
  1. Psilocybin survives its own control. Stable in direction and significance
     across all five sensitivity re-fits (OR range 1.60–2.77). Both estimators
     (conditional logit and the unmodelled paired test) agree in sign. Because
     extraction noise is non-differential it biases toward the null, so this is a
     LOWER bound.
  2. Ketamine's advantage vanishes under its own control. Raw 69.4% helped, but
     within-patient OR is indistinguishable from 1 in the primary fit and all five
     re-fits. This is the design catching exactly the failure mode it was built for.
     State it as a null, not as evidence of no effect — the CI admits modest benefit.
  3. The effect flips by symptom class, strongly. Within the same patient these
     substances are reported as helping mood and cognition, and markedly less so
     (often worse) for exertion-related symptoms. Since post-exertional malaise is
     the defining symptom of ME/CFS, this is the most consequential result here.
     The community's own indication map (§2.2) already points the same way.

7. TWO PLANNED SENSITIVITY CHECKS THAT THIS DATASET CANNOT SUPPORT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Both were in the approved plan. Both turned out to be impossible. This is
documented IN the notebook (§5) rather than quietly dropped, because in each case
the field looks usable until you inspect it. Do not re-add them.

  a) CONFIDENCE FILTERING IS VACUOUS FOR THIS ENDPOINT.
     The run README reports 43.2% high / 50.9% medium / 5.8% low, and that is true
     ACROSS FIELDS. But `treatment_outcome` carries the constant value "medium" on
     all 35,505 records that populate it — it is the field's schema-declared tier,
     not a per-record judgement. There is nothing to filter on.
     (This also means the original justification for reading the JSON over the CSV
     was wrong. The JSON is still correct to use — it is the authoritative dataset
     and the CSV is documented as lossy — but the stated reason was fixed.)

  b) BOT EXCLUSION BY TEXT VOLUME IS IMPOSSIBLE.
     record_meta.text_count is 1 for all 69,161 records. The aggregation step wrote
     one synthetic document per patient and stored the count of documents, not of
     underlying posts/comments. No text-volume signal survives into this file.
     Mitigation: a bot like AutoModerator enters a patient-fixed-effects model as
     one stratum out of 745, so its leverage is negligible anyway. Substituted a
     drop-extreme-treatment-count fit as the closest available proxy.

Replacement sensitivity set (five re-fits, all in §5): ambiguous strings included;
patients naming <=3 treatments; drop >140 treatments/patient; symptom-specified
triples only; excluding r/covidlonghaulers posters.

Honest wrinkles in the sensitivity table, already written into §7:
  - ketamine flips direction (OR 0.73) in the light-stacker fit, but is never
    significant in any fit.
  - LSD reaches nominal significance below 1 in ONE fit (symptom-specified only,
    OR 0.47, p=0.025) and is contradicted by the others (range 0.27–1.13). That is
    one exploratory uncorrected fit among five. Do not promote it to a finding.

12. HARD CONSTRAINTS — carry these into any follow-up
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

From data/full_corpus_2026-07-31/README.md. These are not stylistic preferences.

  - COHORT-LEVEL CLAIMS ONLY. Two identical extraction passes agreed on the field
    set for 36.5% of records and on values 58.8% of the time. Aggregates are stable
    to 0.8pp; a single record is one draw. No case studies. No per-patient
    narratives. No quoting an individual's extracted values as their story.
  - NO TIME AXIS. No before/after, no dose-response over time, no trend claims.
  - NO DENOMINATOR FOR SILENT FAILURE. People post about treatments that did
    something; a drug tried once and quietly abandoned never appears. The
    within-patient design removes REPORTER bias. It does not remove SELECTION INTO
    REPORTING, and nothing in this dataset can.
  - NO EFFICACY CLAIM. Self-report, no blinding, no randomisation, no control over
    what else the patient was doing. A within-patient contrast is not a trial.
  - NO DOSING CLAIM. The `dosage` field is populated per patient but never linked
    to a specific drug — a stated "0.25 g" cannot be attributed to the mushrooms
    rather than to something else in the stack. §4.4 is stratification, not dosing.
  - THIS IS NOT THE DRUG-SENTIMENT PIPELINE. treatment_outcome comes from variable
    extraction. src/run_sentiment_pipeline.py has NEVER been run on this corpus.
    These rates are not comparable to `sentiment` numbers from other PatientPunk
    runs — different measurement, different units, different failure modes.
  - EVERY PROPORTION GETS A WILSON CI AND ITS DENOMINATOR. No bare percentages.
    Harm counts are reported as counts, never as rates.
