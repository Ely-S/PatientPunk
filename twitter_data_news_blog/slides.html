Why it matters

When a drug trends, is that patients
or the news?

PatientPunk reads patient posts to learn which treatments people try and how those treatments work out. If news coverage drives the volume on a platform, then on that platform:

01

Raw volume says little about how many patients use a drug.

02

Trends in sentiment or uptake track the news calendar.

03

Posts written during a spike come from a different crowd.

So we tested whether X really is more news-driven than Reddit.

The data

Two platforms, five years

0X posts that name both a drug and long COVID, from 32,515 accounts
0LDN (low-dose naltrexone) posts in X's own complete daily count, which has no gaps
0posts and comments: the whole of r/covidlonghaulers
Account-day

One account mentioning a drug on one day. We count these, not posts, so one busy account, thread or bot can't fake a surge.

Spike

A day with at least 3× the median of the 28 days around it, and too high to be chance (under 1 in 1,000).

Step

A lasting change in a platform's level that doesn't fall back once the news fades.

Volatility, √α

How far interest swings from day to day around its monthly level, after removing counting noise. √α = 0.3 means about ±30% on a typical day.

The samples differ. Every X post had to name long COVID; Reddit posts didn't, because the whole subreddit is about it. We compare both platforms over the same 1,929 days, 1 August 2020 to 11 November 2025, and return to this sampling difference at the end.

Main result

X is more volatile for 18 of 24 drugs

● X  ● Reddit  √α: day-to-day swings in attention after removing the monthly level and counting noise. Hover over a row for values.

Median ratio of day-to-day volatility (α), X to Reddit: 1.62 (95% CI 1.20–2.62; 1.27 on the chart's √α scale).

Both platforms agree on which drugs are news drugs (ρ = 0.61): metformin, HBOT (hyperbaric oxygen) and Paxlovid. Staples such as zinc, quercetin and antihistamines stay steady on both platforms.

Much of the gap comes from single headlines shared word for word by many accounts. Counting each headline once cuts the ratio to 1.28, with an interval that includes 1.

Nine dated releases

X jumps; Reddit has an ordinary week

● X  ● Reddit  Account-days (distinct accounts each day, summed) in the first week, relative to the four weeks before (log scale). Hollow, faded rows concerned acute COVID, so most posts about them fall outside the data.

Every LDN and Paxlovid release lifted X 3–10× in its first week. Reddit rose only 1.1–1.4×, except after Reuters.

For Paxlovid, X reacted on the same scale to two null trials (9.1× and 4.3×) as to a positive study (8.6×). On Reddit, the following weeks rose after the positive news and fell after the null results.

X reacts to whether something is news. The patient community may react to what the news says.

This is a hand-picked case series, not a random sample, and nine events can't separate a delayed response from coincidence.

Who posts during a spike

Spike-day posts say less about patients

On spike days, fewer X posts become treatment reports or describe the poster's own use.

The drop in treatment reports remains after removing flagged bot posts.

On X, people who first post about a drug on a spike day are less likely to post about it again: 11% versus 15% (odds ratio 0.64).

Bots, marketers and copy-paste

Bots and marketers don't cause spikes, but they inflate treatment reports

  • Bots and marketers post less on spike days: 2.6% of posts, versus 4.7% on ordinary days nearby. Remove them and 220 of 225 spikes remain.
  • Headline relays, one link shared by many ordinary accounts, swell the spikes: 18.8% of posts on spike days versus 6.6% on ordinary days nearby.
  • 141 of 2,186 ivermectin reports on X come from flagged posts, and all are positive: 105 vendor scripts, which the pipeline reads as testimonials, and 36 from accounts repeating themselves. These are flagged, not confirmed fakes.
  • Removing them barely moves ivermectin's positive share (91.9% to 91.3%). For a drug with mixed reviews, the same campaign would matter much more.
Limits of the analysis

What this can't tell you

Platform or population?

Reddit is a support group; X is a public square. We can't separate a platform's design from who is in the room.

Observational data

This is not an experiment. News events were identified from word patterns and a hand-picked list, and we chose the peak phrases after reading the peaks.

Other causes

Reddit's LDN discussion was already rising in September 2022, so the Reuters story can't take credit for the whole step.

Fixed windows

Returns and steps are measured over fixed windows, such as eight weeks before and after. Other windows would shift the exact figures; we didn't tune them.

Limitations of the data

The raw material has gaps

280 characters

The scrape stored only each post's 280-character preview, with no conversation or parent ids. Keyword matching ran on the preview, so long posts were missed: all 3 misses in an LDN test week were long posts.

64 vs 51

LDN posts on Reuters day in the scrape and in X's complete count. The queries used different long COVID terms, and the count, run later, probably can't see deleted posts.

6 of 102

posts we marked as organic turned out to be one-off promotion or spam. Real marketing on X could be up to three times what we flagged.

One labeller

All 220 labels used to check the bot flags came from one AI model, with no independent human check. Treatment reports also come from language models.

UTC days

A story that breaks in the US evening spans two UTC days, which spreads X spikes across neighbouring days and makes single-day ratios conservative.

13 of 50

sampled X authors whose posts became LDN reports never said they had long COVID or any other condition, in their bio or their posts. Naming long COVID in a post doesn't make someone a patient: parents, clinicians and vaccine-injury accounts also pass the filter.

Sampling issues

The two samples were drawn differently

Each square is 1% of the LDN posts from 70 X accounts. Blue: posts already in our corpus. Grey (79%): posts our corpus missed, almost all because they don't name long COVID.

Every X post had to name a drug and long COVID. Reddit posts only had to name the drug, because the whole subreddit is about long COVID.

Headlines name the condition; patients discussing their dose, side effects or crashes often don't. So the X corpus over-represents headlines and under-represents everyday treatment talk.

The 70 accounts were hand-picked, so 79% shows the direction of the gap, not its size. This bias probably raises X's measured volatility, but it doesn't explain the gap: requiring Reddit posts to name long COVID too widens X's lead.

32% vs 66%of LDN posts describe the poster's or someone else's use, on X and on Reddit (samples of 2,273 and 500 posts)
1,083 vs 12,040positive, negative or mixed LDN reports across the whole scrape, from 554 X authors and 2,634 Reddit authors
60 of 152patients posting about LDN in an X sample had no post in our corpus. Patients who name long COVID only in their bio can't be found at all, because our API tier can't search bios.
Lessons for future scrapes

What we would do differently next time

1Store and search the full post.

Request note_tweet, keep its text, and run keyword matching on the full body. Keep conversation and parent ids so replies can be read in context.

2Search by author, not just by keyword.

Find posts that name the drug and the condition, then pull each author's posts about the drug: 4.8× as many posts for 70 authors. Pool authors across every drug to reach those who never name both together.

3Pull author profiles and label the condition.

Add expansions=author_id with user fields, then flag bios that mention long COVID, ME/CFS or POTS. User fields also give each account's creation date, one of our few bot signals.

4Widen the term lists.

Add brand names, abbreviations, misspellings and non-English forms, plus more long COVID and ME/CFS terms. Add one query without the condition term, so news about acute COVID isn't lost.

5Pair every scrape with a complete count.

X's daily counts have no gaps. Run them at the same time and with the same terms as the scrape, before posts are deleted, and match the query rules across platforms.

6Fix the rules first, and check the labels.

Write down which news releases qualify before looking. Have a person check a sample of every model's labels, and save the script behind every number.

Budget before pulling timelines: X charges $0.005 per post read and $0.10 per counts request.

For anyone mining X for patient reports

What to do

1

Flag spike windows and check every estimate with and without them.

2

Count distinct accounts and texts, not posts. Remove vendor scripts before quoting sentiment.

3

Use X to see which direction interest in a drug is heading, not how widely it is used.

4

On Reddit, look for lasting steps, then split them into first-time and returning posters.

5

Expect politicised drugs (ivermectin, hydroxychloroquine, IVIG) to be noisy on every platform.

In short: X spikes with the news and fades. When news does reach patients, which is rare, it shows up on Reddit as a smaller, slower step that can last.
